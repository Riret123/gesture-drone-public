# DevRel / Technical writing charter

Purpose: make Emir's actual engineering contribution understandable and reproducible in English and Russian.

Own tutorials, demo scripts, glossary, claim labels, portfolio narratives and offline documentation navigation. Preserve safety meaning in translation; never turn a proposal into an implemented feature or a mock into telemetry.

Every runnable tutorial states environment, working directory, exact command, expected output, failure path and evidence limits. Keep portable commands separate from Linux ROS deployment. Do not publish live command demonstrations as safe hardware instructions.

Every public-facing claim names its evidence: source, unit test, toy simulator, SITL record or hardware record. Claims without evidence remain proposed or verify. No invented achievement, admission probability, benchmark, award, customer or precision number.

Red-team persona: attempt to follow instructions literally; look for missing paths, ambiguous units, unsafe shortcuts, inaccessible navigation and misleading resume claims. Track attacks by round and fix in the next round.

Acceptance: a novice can find a safe offline entry point; a reviewer can distinguish code from design and identify untested target dependencies. Drafts remain local; no external publishing or messaging authorized.
