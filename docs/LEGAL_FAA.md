# FAA checklist — verify, not legal advice

Reviewed against linked FAA guidance during this run (2026-11-08). Recheck official
rules before each actual operation; this is an incomplete planning aid for the US.

- [ ] Determine the purpose of each flight. Do not assume an educational or portfolio
  project qualifies as recreation. Review the recreational exception and Part 107
  with a qualified operator. [FAA recreational guidance](https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers).
- [ ] For recreational operations, complete TRUST and carry proof as required.
  [FAA TRUST](https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers/knowledge_test_updates).
- [ ] For Part 107 operations, verify remote pilot certification, aircraft eligibility,
  operating rules and required authorizations with the responsible pilot.
  [FAA getting started](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started).
- [ ] Verify registration for the actual aircraft, operation type and takeoff weight.
  Part 107 aircraft require registration; the recreational weight exception is limited.
  [FAA registration](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone).
- [ ] Verify Remote ID compliance or applicable authorized exception, including FRIA
  conditions. Do not assume a DIY FC or companion provides compliant Remote ID.
  [FAA Remote ID](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/remote_id).
- [ ] Check airspace, restrictions, visual line of sight, people, night operation,
  local permissions and any necessary waivers/authorization for the specific plan.
  [FAA LAANC](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc).
- [ ] Obtain campus/property-owner launch/landing permission and review institution
  policies, insurance, privacy and local restrictions. No campus or location has been
  identified here, so no specific campus approval or policy is verified.

Gesture, voice, Quest or automated landing does not remove the pilot's obligations.
No legal determination, operational approval or hardware flight authorization here.
